# ADR-004: Verified Pipeline Design

- **Status:** Proposed
- **Date:** 2026-11-02
- **Scope:** the new `src/backend/priscianus/pipeline/` package, `priscianus.verifier`, `priscianus.llm.client`, and the `tools` and `verified` modes in `src/evals/`

## Context

Milestone 3 adds the two modes that plan.md section 2 compares with `raw`: `tools` (the model can call the morphology index) and `verified` (tools, verifier and repair). The before/after table compares them with [baseline-eval.md](../baseline-eval.md), so the definitions must be fixed before the first run, as [ADR-003](ADR-003.md) did for the baseline.

The plan describes repair as sending a contradicted claim back "with the DB's correct parses". The baseline shows why that wording cannot stand: the index contains the gold reading for only 79.3% of test items, and every model beats it.

### What repair would act on

To size the decisions, the stored `raw` claims on the dev split were re-classified with the current verifier and index (build `8081e7a9`, after #58). That is 900 answers: 300 items for each of three models. Only dev was opened at this level of detail.

| First-pass state | Answers | Exact | Wrong | Wrong, gold reading in the index |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| CONTRADICTED, form has readings | 31 | 10 | 21 | 13 |
| CONTRADICTED, form has none | 3 | 0 | 3 | 0 |
| UNKNOWN, inflected claim, form has readings | 11 | 0 | 11 | 6 |
| UNKNOWN, inflected claim, form has none | 9 | 5 | 4 | 0 |
| UNKNOWN, uninflected claim | 87 | 80 | 7 | 0 |
| VERIFIED | 267 | 244 | 23 | 12 |
| AMBIGUOUS | 492 | 391 | 101 | 72 |
| **All** | **900** | **730** | **170** | **103** |

What this shows:

- **Most errors are out of reach.** 124 of the 170 wrong answers (73%) are real readings of the form that fit the index, chosen wrongly or lemmatised differently. No verdict catches them. The list of readings from `lookup_form` might help; repair cannot.
- **Repair's ceiling is small.** The trigger chosen in Decision 4 reaches 32 of the 170 wrong answers. The gold reading is available to the model for 19 of them, which is 2.1 points of pooled exact match at most.
- **A contradiction is often a correct answer.** 10 of the 34 contradicted answers are exact on dev. On test, the committed reports (scored before #58) count 111 of 193 (58%) as index disagreements: Haiku 38 of 75, Sonnet 35 of 59, Opus 38 of 59. Dev has about a third of test's rate (1.1% of answers against 3.7%). I have not looked at why, to keep test out of the tuning, so dev will understate harm.
- **Uninflected homographs are a trap.** All 15 uninflected claims whose form has an inflected homograph in the index are exact. Showing those readings could only hurt.

The 10 exact-but-contradicted answers on dev, by cause:

| Cause | Answers | Examples | Is the contradiction right? |
|---|---:|---|---|
| A participle or derived word has its own lemma, not linked to the verb | 4 | *geniti* (twice; index *genitus*, no `base_lemma`), *oppositum*, *conplexus* | No: index gap |
| Personal pronouns: the index stores no gender | 3 | *ego*, *te*, *tu* | No: index gap |
| Lemma spelling | 1 | *muttis* (index *muttio*, gold *mutio*) | No: index gap |
| The model added a feature that the gold does not mark | 2 | *rogare* with `Mood=Ind`, *discursus* (noun) with `Voice=Pass` | Yes, though the score ignores it |

These are raw claims, so they only approximate what `tools` will produce. They justify the policy and set expectations; they do not predict the result.

## Decision 1: Claim schema

Every structured model output, in the parse task now and the explain and drill tasks in M4, has this shape:

```json
{
  "text": "",
  "claims": [
    {
      "form": "loquitur",
      "lemma": "loquor",
      "upos": "VERB",
      "features": {
        "Case": "None", "Number": "Sing", "Gender": "None", "Person": "3", "Mood": "Ind",
        "Tense": "Pres", "Aspect": "None", "Voice": "Pass", "VerbForm": "Fin", "Degree": "None"
      }
    }
  ]
}
```

- **Every feature is required, with `"None"` for one the word lacks.** This keeps the `raw-parse-v3` rule: when features were optional, Haiku left them out. `"None"` is removed when parsing, so it never reaches the verifier or the store.
- **Values** are the canonical values of ADR-003: the values in `morphology/ud_features.py`, minus `Degree=Pos`, plus `VerbForm=Gdv`. A test asserts that they equal `raw_prompt.ALLOWED_FEATURES`.
- **`text`** is prose for the learner in M4. In the parse task it is empty on the first pass and one sentence of reason on a repair turn, which helps when checking harm items by hand.
- **Parse task rule:** exactly one claim, whose `key_loose(form)` equals the target's. Anything else is a schema failure, counted and never retried (ADR-003). Otherwise a model could correct the spelling of the form and verify a different word.

### Where it lives

| Option | Benefits | Downsides |
|---|---|---|
| **A. `priscianus/verifier/claims.py`** | The verifier already owns the idea of a claim. Mypy strict. Everything that needs it can already depend on `verifier`. | The module also holds a JSON schema generator, which is not verification. |
| **B. In `priscianus/llm/`** | Next to the API call. | The deterministic verifier would import from the LLM package, and `llm/` is not mypy strict. |
| **C. In `priscianus/pipeline/`** | Next to its main user. | The verifier would depend on the package that depends on it. |

**Decision: option A.** It holds frozen dataclasses `Claim(form, lemma, upos, features)` and `ModelOutput(text, claims)`, plus `SchemaError(reason)`, `output_schema()` and `parse_output(text) -> ModelOutput | SchemaError`. Dataclasses match the rest of the backend. The M4 API can wrap them in Pydantic at its boundary.

### The existing claim types

| Type | What happens to it |
|---|---|
| `verifier.classify.Claim` | Replaced by the shared type. `classify` still ignores `upos`: index parts of speech are Wiktionary's, and ADR-003's verdicts do not use it. Only the repair trigger reads it (Decision 4). |
| `evals.raw_prompt.Claim` | Removed. `parse_claim` builds the shared type, taking `form` from the gold token because `raw-parse-v3` output has none. `score_claim` takes the shared type. |
| `raw_prompt.OUTPUT_SCHEMA` | **Stays a literal.** It is part of the frozen `raw-parse-v3` request, so generating it would risk changing the request hash. |

## Decision 2: Tools

Two tools. Result content is compact JSON with sorted keys, so that the same lookup always gives the same request and cache key.

### `lookup_form`

Input: `{"form": string}`. It returns every reading of the form, merged as the verifier merges them, so the model and the verifier see the same list:

```json
{"form":"loquitur","readings":[{"deponent":true,"features":"Mood=Ind|Number=Sing|Person=3|Tense=Pres|VerbForm=Fin|Voice=Act","form":"loquitur","lemma":"loquor","pos":"verb"}],"total":1,"truncated":false}
```

- `features` is a UD FEATS string in canonical form (a gerundive shows as `VerbForm=Gdv`, the convention the prompt teaches, not the index's `Part|Tense=Fut|Voice=Pass`).
- `lemma` is the display lemma. `base_lemma` appears when the index links the lemma to a verb or positive. `deponent` and `variant` appear only when true.
- `pos` is the index's own tag (`verb`, `noun`, `name`, ...), not a UPOS.

### `inflect`

Input: `{"lemma": string, "features": {<feature name>: <canonical value>}, "pos": string (optional)}`, with the names and values from Decision 1, and `"None"` not allowed.

For *amo*, third person plural perfect active indicative:

```json
{"forms":["amāvērunt","amārunt","amāvēre"],"ignored":[],"lemma":"amo","total":3,"truncated":false}
```

The executor has to correct three behaviours of today's `morphology.inflect`, all found by running it:

| Call | Today | Executor rule |
|---|---|---|
| `amor`, `Gender=Masc`; `loquor`, `Voice=Pass` | `NoSuchFormError`, because the index stores no noun gender and tags deponents active | Drop what `relax` drops for the verifier, and name it in `ignored`. |
| `amo`, `VerbForm=Gdv` | `NoSuchFormError`, because the index does not store that encoding | Translate canonical features to the index's encoding. Also needs the lemma links in the follow-ups: *amandus* is a separate lemma today. |
| `is`, `Case=Voc` | Returns `["-"]`, a placeholder for an empty table cell | Filter the placeholder. See the follow-ups: 114,896 of 2,152,654 analyses have the form `-`. |

`MorphologyIndex` gains an `inflect` method. Strict mode on the tool definitions is not used, because the features are optional by nature; the executor validates input and reports errors itself.

### Bounds

**At most 50 readings or forms per result.** A truncated result sets `truncated: true` and keeps the real `total`.

- On dev, 99.3% of distinct forms have 13 readings or fewer. The largest are *se* (48) and *sibi* (28), both common words, so a lower cap would truncate exactly the forms learners meet.
- With the placeholder filtered, no form in the index has more than 50 readings (*quoi* has 37), so in practice the cap is a safety limit.
- The cap applies only to what the model is shown. `classify` always uses every reading, so it cannot change a verdict.

### Errors

Errors reach the model as a `tool_result` with `is_error: true` and content `{"error": <code>, "message": <text>}`. Input errors never raise into the loop; a failure of the database or the code does, and fails the run.

| Code | When | The message says |
|---|---|---|
| `unknown_form` | No analysis of the form | The index does not hold this form. It is incomplete and holds no uninflected words, so this does not mean that the form is wrong. |
| `unknown_lemma` | `inflect` on a lemma not in the index | The same, for lemmas. |
| `no_such_form` | The lemma exists but has no form with these features | The index has no such form of this lemma, which may be a gap. |
| `invalid_input` | Arguments fail validation, including a form with no letters | What was wrong. |
| `unknown_tool` | Unknown tool name | The available tools. |
| `too_many_calls` | Past the per-round limit (Decision 3) | Call fewer tools at once. |

The tool descriptions state three things the model must not infer: an error does not mean a form is wrong; a feature missing from a reading means the index does not record it there, not that the word lacks it (noun gender, pronoun person); and a deponent's `Voice=Act` is a convention of the index. The second matters most: the gold marks noun gender, and a model copying a reading without it would lose exact match.

### Why `list_lemmas` waits

ADR-001: "the interface grows with its callers." The parse task always starts from a form, so it has no use for `list_lemmas`. Its caller is the M4 drill generator, which will fix its filters, and ADR-003 left open how `freq_band` gets filled. Including it now would lengthen every parse request and fix a signature with nothing to shape it.

## Decision 3: Loop limits

A **round** is one assistant turn containing tool calls, however many, together with their results.

| Limit | Value | Reason |
|---|---:|---|
| Tool rounds on the first pass | 3 | One word needs a lookup, perhaps a second lookup or an `inflect`, and one spare. |
| Tool rounds on each repair attempt | 1 | The readings are already in the repair message. |
| Tool calls per round | 5 | One word. M4's sentence tasks set their own value (a sentence has about 20 words, ADR-001). |
| API calls per item, worst case | 8 | 3 rounds + answer, then 2 attempts of 1 round + answer. |

These values are starting points, not measurements. They are checked on dev, and belong to the versioned prompt (Decision 7).

### At the limit

| Option | Benefits | Downsides |
|---|---|---|
| **A. Return a typed failure** (as #72 sketches). | Simplest. | The item has no answer, though the model has all the evidence it asked for. A learner gets nothing. It penalises `tools` for looping rather than for being wrong. |
| **B. One last call with `tool_choice: {"type": "none"}`**, so that the model must answer from what it has. | The item keeps a real answer. The call is bounded. | One more API call. It invalidates the prompt cache for the messages, but not for tools and system. |

**Decision: option B.** The response records `tool_limit_hit`, and reports count it. If that answer is still invalid, it is a schema failure like any other. More than 1% of dev items hitting the limit means the limit is wrong: raise it and bump the prompt version.

### API facts the loop relies on

From the API documentation, as of this ADR:

- Prompt-cache order is tools, then system, then messages, so the marker the client already puts on the system prompt covers the tool definitions. Changing `tool_choice` keeps that cache and invalidates only the messages. Haiku 5.5's minimum cacheable prefix is listed as 512 tokens, and the raw prompt is about 1,950.
- Forced tool use (`any`, `tool`) returns a 400 on Sonnet 5.5 and Opus 5.5, so the prompt asks for the lookup and `tool calls per item` shows whether the model complied. `none` and `auto` are accepted.
- These models think by default, and thinking blocks must be passed back unchanged. History is **append-only**: the loop and the repair turns append each response's full `content`, never just its text.
- The documentation does not explicitly say that `output_config.format` combines with client tools, and I have not run it. **The first step of #72 is one live call to confirm it.** If it fails, the fallback is a final call with `tool_choice: none` and the schema, after the tool rounds.

`thinking`, effort, temperature and `max_tokens` (4096) stay as in `raw`, so that the modes differ only in what they are meant to compare.

## Decision 4: Repair policy

### What triggers repair

A claim enters repair if **all** of these hold:

1. Its verdict is CONTRADICTED or UNKNOWN.
2. Its `upos` is not an uninflected tag (`ADV`, `ADP`, `CCONJ`, `SCONJ`, `PART`, `INTJ`). The index does not hold those words by design (ADR-003).
3. The form has at least one reading. Otherwise there is no evidence to send, and asking again only applies pressure.

| Option | Reaches (dev) | Exact answers put at risk (dev) | Assessment |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| CONTRADICTED only (the plan) | 24 wrong | 10 | Misses the UNKNOWN answers that have evidence. |
| CONTRADICTED and every UNKNOWN | 46 wrong | 95 | Mostly uninflected words, correct and with nothing useful to show. |
| **The rule above** | **32 wrong** | **10** | All 11 added answers were wrong, with none put at risk: the model named a lemma the index lacks for a form it knows (*restare*, *nuntiare*, *deperio* for *depereo*). |

AMBIGUOUS and VERIFIED claims never enter repair: the index has confirmed them, and it has no evidence about which reading the sentence wants.

**Omitted features are not a trigger.** baseline-eval.md's third hypothesis expected a required-features check to help. On dev, 30 verified or ambiguous answers leave out a feature that every matching reading agrees on, and 20 of them are already exact, because the gold does not mark that feature. The prompt and the required `"None"` address omission instead.

### The repair message

One user turn, appended after the model's answer, with the same schema and tools. The template, tuned on dev only:

```
The morphology index does not confirm this claim about "{form}":
  lemma {lemma}, {features}
{for CONTRADICTED: The index has the lemma, but not this reading of the form.}
{for UNKNOWN: The index does not hold the lemma "{lemma}".}

The index's readings of "{form}":
{each reading, as lookup_form gives it}

The index comes from Wiktionary and does not read the sentence. It is incomplete: it can lack a
real reading, and it can give a participle its own lemma. Your answer may be right and the
index wrong.

Read the sentence again. If one of these readings or another parse fits it better, answer with
that. If your parse is right, give the same claim again. Do not change it only because the index
disagrees. Keep the lemma conventions. In "text", say in one sentence why you changed or kept it.
```

It lists every reading (no cap), never says the claim is wrong, and never mentions gold.

### Retries and stopping

At most **2 repair attempts**. Each attempt re-classifies every claim in the new output, and stops early when:

- no claim needs repair; or
- the model **restates** the claim (same lemma key and same canonical features). A second attempt would add no information, only pressure; or
- the response fails the schema, in which case the previous valid claims stand and the failure is recorded on the attempt.

### Final state

The final verdict is computed on the final claim, and gives each claim a state for the UI and the report:

| Final verdict | State | The learner sees (M4) |
|---|---|---|
| VERIFIED | `verified` | Verified |
| AMBIGUOUS | `ambiguous` | Verified, with the other readings |
| UNKNOWN | `unverifiable` | Not in the database |
| CONTRADICTED | `disputed` | The claim, marked as disagreeing with the database, with its readings |

**A claim still contradicted is flagged, not dropped.** On test, more than half of contradicted answers are correct (58% on the raw runs), so dropping would remove more right answers than wrong ones. `disputed` is a fourth state beyond plan.md's three badges, and M4 has to design for it.

## Decision 5: The index as an oracle

The question is how to stop repair from pushing correct answers towards the index's gaps.

| Option | Benefits | Downsides |
|---|---|---|
| **A. Overwrite the claim with an index reading.** | No API call. | Undefined when the form has several readings. Certain harm whenever the gold is not in the index. |
| **B. Closed choice:** "this is wrong; pick one of these" (the plan's wording). | Clears contradictions reliably. | When the gold is not in the index, no right answer is on offer: 7 of the 10 exact answers on dev are in that position. |
| **C. Evidence with the right to disagree.** The readings are evidence, the model decides, and restating is a valid answer. | The only option where a correct answer can survive. The model can use the sentence, which the index cannot. | A model may still defer to the index. That is measured, as repair harm. |
| **D. No repair: verify and flag only.** | No harm is possible, and no extra calls. | Fixes nothing. |

**Decision: option C, with D as the fallback** and these guards:

1. **The message frames the index as fallible** (Decision 4), and the claim is never replaced by code.
2. **No repair without coverage or evidence** (the trigger rule).
3. **Known gaps are closed, not repaired around.** The verifier no longer checks `Gender` or `Number` on a pronoun reading that lacks them. This applies ADR-003's rule that a feature the reading lacks is not checked to the case where it is known to be a gap, and on dev it clears *ego*, *te* and *tu*, with no wrong answer cleared. The rule stays strict elsewhere, because it catches real errors (`Voice` on a noun, `Mood` on an infinitive). It lives in `features_agree`, so `index-agreement` is re-run and its ceiling restated in the same change. Lemma links are in the follow-ups.
4. **A flag is the outcome of disagreement**, not a failure (Decision 4).
5. **A stop rule, fixed now.** After the dev runs of `verified`, pooled over the models: **repair stays only if repair success is at least twice repair harm.** Otherwise `verified` on test runs with repair off, as verify and flag. The factor of 2 is a judgement: a harm item is worse than an unrepaired one, because it ends with a badge saying *verified*, and a repaired item costs extra API calls. With so few events on dev this is a coarse check, and it is applied once.
6. **Every harm item is listed and checked by hand** (#77). A cause in the index is fixed in the build, as ADR-003 requires, and never in the eval.

Test is run after this and is not used to change the policy. Since dev understates index disagreement, test harm is the first real measurement, and is reported as it comes out.

## Decision 6: Metrics for the new modes

ADR-003's metrics apply unchanged to the final claim. Exact match means what the code and baseline-eval.md mean: lemma, part-of-speech class and every gold-marked feature.

**Same verifier for every row.** Verdict rates depend on the verifier and the index, and the published ones are already out of date: re-classifying dev gives 34 contradicted answers against the 39 published, because of #58. So the before/after table re-scores `raw` with the same verifier and index build as the new modes (no API calls), and shows the published figure beside it. Exact match does not use the verifier and stays as published.

### Repair

Over items in `verified`, comparing the **first-pass** claim with the **final** claim, both scored against the gold:

| Metric | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Items entering repair** | Items with a claim that meets the Decision 4 trigger on the first pass ÷ items |
| **Repair success** | Items in repair that are not exact on the first pass and exact in the final claim. Given as a count, ÷ items in repair, and in points ÷ all items. |
| **Repair harm** | Items in repair that are exact on the first pass and not exact in the final claim. Given the same three ways. |
| **Net repair effect** | (Success − harm) ÷ all items. Equals the difference in exact match between `verified` and its own first pass. |
| **Repair clearance** | Items in repair whose verdict goes from CONTRADICTED or UNKNOWN to VERIFIED or AMBIGUOUS ÷ items in repair. **Not a measure of quality.** |
| **Restated** | Items in repair where the model kept its claim ÷ items in repair |

The report also shows the full table of exact and not exact, before and after.

**Hallucination must never be read alone.** A harm item goes from CONTRADICTED to VERIFIED, so deferring to the index lowers the hallucination rate while accuracy falls. Any report of hallucination in `verified` gives repair harm and exact match beside it. The target from baseline-eval.md (below 2% on items whose form is in the index) is judged that way.

### Residual error

The plan's "errors that reach the learner". For each answered item, by final state:

| | Exact | Not exact |
|---|---|---|
| `verified`, `ambiguous` | Correct | **False assurance** |
| `unverifiable` | Correct, unchecked | **Unchecked error** |
| `disputed` | **False alarm** | Caught error |

| Metric | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Residual error** | (False assurance + unchecked error) ÷ items: wrong and not marked as disputed. |
| **False-alarm rate** | False alarms ÷ items. Always reported beside residual error, which could otherwise be driven to zero by flagging everything. |

`raw` and `tools` have no flag, so their residual error is every wrong answer, which is 100% minus exact match when there are no schema failures. The four cells are also given for `raw` and `tools`, re-scored, to show what flagging alone would do. This is the option D row, and needs no API calls.

An item with a schema failure has no claim, so it is not in the table. It is counted on its own and fails exact match, as in ADR-003.

### Cost and latency

| Metric | Definition | In `raw` |
|---|---|---|
| **Tool calls per item** | `tool_use` blocks executed, including errors. Mean, p95, max. | 0 |
| **API calls per item** | Calls to `LLMClient.complete` that returned, including cache hits and excluding retried attempts. Mean, p95, max. | 1 |
| **$ per item** | The **list cost** of the item's calls, summed: what the calls cost when first made, including prompt-cache pricing and thinking, with the eval's own cache ignored. | Same, so $0.17 per 1,000 for Haiku test stands. Only a few cache hits differ (1 or 2 per run). |
| **p50/p95 latency per item** | The sum over the item's calls of the latency when first made, plus tool execution. Includes the client's retries and back-off. | One call, so the same as published. |

List cost is needed because `verified`'s first pass is a cache hit on the `tools` run (Decision 7), which records $0 and no latency. Without it, `verified` would look cheaper and faster than `tools`. Actual spend is reported separately, for budgeting.

### Noise floor

The baseline has no estimate of run-to-run variance. Comparing `raw` with `tools` item by item would count ordinary resampling as harm or gain. #78 re-runs `raw` on dev once with `sample=1` for Haiku and Sonnet (about $0.56) to measure how many items flip by chance. Repair harm needs no such correction in the same way, since both of its claims come from one conversation, though the repair response itself is one sample.

## Decision 7: Prompts

| Option | Benefits | Downsides |
|---|---|---|
| **A. Separate first-pass prompts** for the two modes. | Each can be tuned alone. | `tools` and `verified` then differ by more than repair, and two samples have to be compared. |
| **B. One first-pass request for both**, with repair instructions only in the repair turn. | `verified`'s first pass is byte-identical to `tools`, so it replays from the response cache. The comparison is **paired**: the only difference is repair, with no sampling noise and no extra cost. | Cost and latency need the list-cost rule above. Repair instructions cannot be in the system prompt. |

**Decision: option B.**

| ID | Pins | Used by |
|---|---|---|
| `tools-parse-v1` | The system prompt, the output schema, the tool definitions, the first-pass limits | `--mode tools`, and the first pass of `--mode verified` |
| `verified-parse-v1` | `tools-parse-v1`, the repair template, the repair limits and the trigger rule | `--mode verified` |

- `tools-parse-v1` starts from `raw-parse-v3`: the same lemma and feature conventions and the same user message. It adds the wrapper of Decision 1; an instruction to call `lookup_form` on the target before answering; and the statement that the index is incomplete, ignores the sentence, may give a participle its own lemma, and that the answer need not be one of its readings. It never mentions gold.
- **Tuned on dev only**, and frozen before test. The prompt changes and their dev scores go in the PR (#75, #76).
- **Versioned as `raw-parse-v3` is.** A change to anything an ID pins bumps the ID. Tool descriptions and limits live in the product code, so a snapshot test over the full request fails if they change without a bump.

Prompts for the parse task live in `evals/`, because the parse task is an eval. Tool definitions and the repair template live in `priscianus/pipeline/`, because the product uses them.

## Decision 8: Code location

| Option | Benefits | Downsides |
|---|---|---|
| **A. A new `priscianus/pipeline/` package.** | The verifier stays what plan.md says it is, "deterministic, no LLM". The dependencies run one way. | One more package. |
| **B. The tool code in `llm/`, the repair loop in `verifier/`** (as the titles of #71 to #73 suggest). | No new package. | `verifier` would call the LLM; `llm` would depend on the index. |

**Decision: option A.**

```
src/backend/priscianus/
  verifier/
    claims.py        # Claim, ModelOutput, output_schema, parse_output (Decision 1)
    classify.py      # takes the shared Claim
    repair.py        # needs_repair, final_state, is_restated: deterministic, no LLM
  llm/
    client.py        # gains tool_choice, and keeps latency on the cache row
  pipeline/
    tools.py         # tool definitions and the executor (Decision 2)
    loop.py          # run_with_tools (Decision 3)
    verify.py        # verify_and_repair and the repair template (Decision 4)
src/evals/evals/
  tools_prompt.py    # tools-parse-v1 and verified-parse-v1
```

Dependencies: `pipeline` → `llm`, `verifier`, `morphology`; `verifier` → `morphology`; `llm` → nothing here; `evals` → all of them.

**Mypy strict:** `verifier.*` (as now, including the new modules), `morphology.*`, and `priscianus.pipeline.*` (added to `pyproject.toml`). `llm/` stays non-strict because of the SDK's dynamic types; `LLMResponse.content` is an explicit `dict[str, Any]` at the boundary. `evals` is already strict.

## Decision 9: Store changes

Additive only, in the way `latency_ms` was added, so that existing raw runs load unchanged. Each existing row is the first and final attempt of its response.

```
-- Changed
runs            + index_build_hash, params_json
responses       + final_state, tool_limit_hit
claims          + attempt (0 = first pass, n = repair attempt), is_final
verdicts        + index_build_hash, readings_json
scores          + claim_id
costs           + attempt, ordinal, list_usd, list_latency_ms

-- New
tool_calls(id, response_id, attempt, round, ordinal, name, input_json, result_json, is_error)
repair_attempts(id, response_id, attempt, message_text, raw_text, format_error,
                stop_reason)   -- cleared | restated | schema_failure | limit
```

- **`index_build_hash` on `runs` is required for the new modes.** Tool results depend on the index, so `start_run` refuses to resume a run under a different build.
- **Verdicts are keyed by build,** as ADR-003 Decision 8 specified and the store does not yet do. This also lets `raw` be re-scored without overwriting its verdicts.
- **`scores` gets a row for each stage,** so that success and harm are queries over stored rows.
- **`EvalStore.run_rows` must aggregate.** It joins `costs` and `claims` one to one, which would multiply rows on a multi-call response. Rows for reports use the final claim and summed costs.
- **The client's `response_cache` gains `latency_ms`**, and a cache hit returns the original cost and latency, so that `costs` can fill `list_usd` and `list_latency_ms`.
- `python -m evals score --run <id>` (#74; not built yet) re-verifies stored claims without API calls. On a `verified` run after an index rebuild it can re-judge the final claims but cannot replay the repair path that a different index would have taken, so the report marks those verdicts as re-scored, and a fresh number needs a new run.

## Consequences

- **The best case for repair is modest:** about 2 points of exact match on dev, if tools change nothing. Most of the gain in M3 has to come from `tools`, and the largest error class, a wrong choice between real readings, may remain. "Small model + verifier ≈ big model raw" is still an open question.
- Hallucination improves by construction when a model defers to the index, so it stops being the headline for `verified`. The headline is exact match, repair harm and residual error together.
- `verified` and `tools` share first-pass responses, so they are not independent samples. That is the intent, and reports say so.
- The worst case rises from 1 API call per item to 8. The averages should be far lower, since most claims do not enter repair, but the cost is unmeasured until #75.
- Dev has few contradiction events (34 answers pooled), so tuning repair on it is coarse, and the stop rule can be decided by a handful of items.
- The verifier change in Decision 5 moves verdicts in the raw runs and raises the index-agreement ceiling. That is why `raw` is re-scored, with the published figures kept beside it.
- The UI gets a fourth state, `disputed`.

### Follow-up issues to open

Both change the index, so both belong **before the first scored `tools` run**, which records the build:

1. **Remove the placeholder rows.** 114,896 analyses (5.3%) have the form `-`, from empty cells in Wiktionary tables, across 6,592 lemmas. `inflect("is", {"Case": "Voc"})` returns `["-"]` today. The drill generator would show these to learners. Removing them changes the row count, not any real form.
2. **Link participles and gerundives to their verbs.** 1,580 verb lemmas have only participle forms and no `base_lemma` (*genitus*, *amandus*). It is the largest cause of index disagreement on dev (4 of 10), and with tools it gets worse: the model is shown `lemma: genitus` when the convention wants *gigno*. `inflect` should follow the same links.
